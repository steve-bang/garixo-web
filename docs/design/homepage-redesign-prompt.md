# Garixo homepage redesign — AI design prompt

Paste everything below the line into an AI design/image tool (Claude Artifacts, v0, Figma AI, Midjourney/Ideogram, etc.).

---

**Role:** You are a senior product designer for B2B SaaS sold to small US businesses. I am the CEO of Garixo, garage management software for independent auto repair shops. Redesign our homepage.

**Audience:** US independent garage owners, ages 35–60. They are mechanics who became business owners — NOT tech people. They are busy, on the shop floor, often on a phone or an older desktop. They distrust jargon, "AI"/"platform" buzzwords, and anything that looks like enterprise software. They want to know in 5 seconds: what is it, will it make me money, is it hard to set up, what does it cost.

**Goal:** One page that makes a shop owner click "Start Free Trial". Professional, calm, trustworthy, and simple. Closer to Stripe/Basecamp/QuickBooks in clarity than to a flashy startup.

**Output:** ONE high-fidelity desktop mockup image of the full homepage, 1440px wide, tall single-page scroll, light mode. Real, readable copy (no lorem ipsum, no gibberish text). English, US spelling, US dollars, US phone/address formats where shown.

**Page structure (top to bottom, nothing else):**
1. Header — logo "Garixo", 4 links max (Features, Pricing, Blog, Log in), one primary button "Start Free Trial".
2. Hero — plain-language headline (max 10 words) about knowing your shop's real profit every day without paperwork; one-sentence subhead; primary CTA "Start Free Trial" + secondary text link "See how it works"; small reassurance line (e.g. "14-day free trial · No credit card · Set up in 15 minutes"). Right side: one clean, realistic product screenshot (work-order board or daily profit summary) with big readable numbers.
3. Three-benefit strip — 3 icons + 3 short lines in owner language: "Know what each job earns", "Keep every car moving", "Get paid faster".
4. How it works — 3 numbered steps, each one sentence (Add the car → Assign the job → Send the invoice).
5. Feature highlights — 4 cards max (Work orders, Customers & vehicles, Parts & inventory, Invoices), each: icon, 3–5 word title, one short sentence.
6. Pricing teaser — 3 plans, clear $ per month, one highlighted, link to full pricing.
7. Testimonial — one quote with a real-looking name, shop name and city (placeholder, mark as sample).
8. FAQ — 4 plain-English questions, collapsed accordions.
9. Final CTA band — one headline, one button.
10. Footer — minimal: product links, legal links, support email.

**Design rules:**
- Layout: single column rhythm, 12-column grid, max content width 1120px, generous whitespace (96–120px between sections), left-aligned text, one idea per section.
- Type: one sans-serif family (Inter or similar). Hero headline 56–64px, section titles 36px, body 18px minimum, line height 1.6. High contrast, WCAG AA or better.
- Color: brand blue (#2563EB range) for primary actions only; slate neutrals for text and surfaces; one warm accent (amber) used sparingly; green only for profit/positive numbers. White and very light gray backgrounds. No gradients, glassmorphism, neon, dark mode, or stock-photo collages.
- Buttons: large (48px+ tall), rounded 8px, one primary style, clear labels (verbs, no "Learn more" everywhere).
- Imagery: real-looking UI screenshots and simple line icons only. If people appear, authentic mechanics in a real US garage — never glossy stock.
- Trust: use only believable, verifiable-sounding claims. Do NOT invent star ratings, review counts, customer counts, or awards.
- Forbidden words: "revolutionary", "AI-powered", "seamless", "leverage", "ecosystem", "all-in-one platform", "next-gen".
- Every section must pass the test: could a 55-year-old shop owner understand it in 3 seconds without help?

**Deliverables:** (1) the desktop mockup image; (2) a 5-line note listing the color hex values, font sizes, and spacing used, so engineering can build it with Tailwind.
