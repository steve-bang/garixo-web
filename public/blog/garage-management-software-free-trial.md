**Quick answer:** A garage management software free trial only tells you something useful if you test it the way your shop actually works — real jobs, your whole team, and the specific tasks that eat the most time today (invoicing, scheduling, parts tracking). Clicking through a demo with sample data will not show you whether the software fits. Below is a practical checklist for what to actually test in the 14 days you get.

Most shop owners sign up for a trial, poke around for ten minutes, and either forget about it or make a decision based on how the login screen looks. That's not a real test — it's a guess with extra steps. (If you haven't started one yet, [Garixo's pricing page](https://www.garixo.com/pricing) has the current trial details.)

## Before You Start: Set a Real Goal for the Trial

Don't start a trial to "see what it's like." Start it to answer one specific question: _does this fix the actual problem pushing me to look for software in the first place?_

If you're not sure what that problem is yet, it helps to name it before you sign up. Common ones worth checking against: [signs your auto repair shop is ready for management software](https://www.garixo.com/blog/signs-auto-repair-shop-needs-management-software), [unbilled labor](https://www.garixo.com/blog/unbilled-labor-auto-repair-shop) quietly draining revenue, or [slow, manual invoicing](https://www.garixo.com/blog/auto-repair-shop-invoice-software). Whatever it is, that's what the trial needs to prove out — not the feature list.

## What to Actually Test in 14 Days

### 1. Create a Real Work Order From Intake to Close

Don't use the sample job the software ships with. Take an actual car that came in this week and run it through the system start to finish: intake, labor and parts added, customer approval, invoice, payment. Time how long each step takes compared to what you do today.

### 2. Get Your Service Writer and at Least One Tech Involved

The owner trying it alone is the most common mistake. If the person who'll actually use it daily never touches it during the trial, you won't know if it works for your shop until you've already paid for it. Have your service writer create a work order. Have a tech update job status from a phone or tablet in the bay.

### 3. Test Invoicing on a Job With Add-Ons

Simple jobs always look clean. Test a job where something got added mid-repair — an extra part, a diagnostic that ran longer than expected. That's exactly the kind of job where unbilled labor and missed charges usually happen, and it's the fastest way to see whether the software actually catches what a paper system would miss.

### 4. Check How Scheduling Handles a Busy Day

Book a full day of appointments the way you actually would, including a same-day walk-in. See how easy it is to see bay availability at a glance and slot someone in without double-booking.

### 5. Look Up a Returning Customer

Pick a customer who's been in before and see how fast you can pull their vehicle history. If the answer takes more than a few seconds, that's a real signal — not a small one.

### 6. Test It on the Device You'll Actually Use

If techs will be updating jobs from a phone in the bay, test it from a phone in the bay — not just a desktop at the front counter. A platform that's clean on a laptop and clunky on mobile will cause real friction once it's live.

---

## What to Ignore During a Trial

Don't spend the 14 days exploring every settings menu or reporting screen you'll rarely open. Focus on the handful of daily tasks that actually run your shop: work orders, invoicing, scheduling, and customer lookup. Everything else is secondary until those four are solid.

---

## Red Flags Worth Watching For

- **Requiring a credit card just to see the product.** Not disqualifying on its own, but worth noting — and worth confirming exactly when you'll be charged if you forget to cancel.
- **A trial that pushes you toward sample data instead of your own.** If it's hard to import your actual customer list or start a real work order quickly, that's often a sign the onboarding process itself is more complicated than it should be.
- **No one on your team wants to keep using it by day 10.** If the trial feels like a chore instead of a relief, that's a real answer — even if the feature list looked good on paper.

---

## After the Trial: Make the Decision With Your Team

Before deciding, ask your service writer and at least one tech directly: would you rather go back to what we were doing before, or keep this? Their answer usually matters more than any feature comparison, because they're the ones who'll be using it every day.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long should a garage management software free trial be?

14 days is common and usually enough if you run real jobs through it rather than just clicking around. Shorter trials can work if you're focused and test with actual work orders from day one.

### Do I need a credit card to start a garage management software free trial?

It depends on the platform. Garixo's 14-day trial doesn't require a credit card. Always check before starting, since some platforms require payment details upfront and auto-bill after the trial ends.

### Should I test a free trial with real jobs or sample data?

Real jobs. Sample data hides the exact friction you're trying to find — how long it actually takes to create a work order, log labor, and invoice a real customer during a busy day.

### What's the biggest mistake shops make during a software trial?

Only having one person test it. If the owner tries it alone but the service writer and technicians never touch it during the trial, you won't find out if the software actually works for your whole team until after you've already committed.

---

## Bottom Line

A free trial is only as useful as the test you put it through. Run real jobs, get your whole team involved, and focus on the specific problem that made you start looking for software in the first place. Fourteen days is enough time to know — as long as you spend them on the right things.
