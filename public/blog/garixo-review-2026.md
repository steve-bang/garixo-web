## What Is Garixo?

Garixo is a web-based platform that runs your shop's daily operations from one place: work orders, invoicing, customer and vehicle records, parts inventory, scheduling, and staff assignments. It's accessible from any browser — a laptop at the front desk, a tablet in the bay, or a phone in your pocket.

It was built specifically for independent shops, not dealership groups. That distinction shapes almost everything about how it works: fewer screens, fewer settings, and a setup that a service writer can learn in a day rather than a week.

If you're not sure whether your shop even needs a system like this yet, [10 signs your auto repair shop is ready for management software](https://www.garixo.com/blog/signs-auto-repair-shop-needs-management-software) is worth reading first.

## Core Features

### Work Orders

Digital work orders replace the clipboard: create a job, assign it to a tech, track status from intake to completion, and close it out without re-entering the same information twice.

### Invoicing

Invoices build directly from the work order — labor, parts, and markup carry over automatically instead of being retyped. Fewer manual steps means fewer places for a charge to quietly get missed.

### Customer and Vehicle Records

Every vehicle has a searchable history: what was done, when, and what was recommended. When a customer calls back three months later, the answer is a lookup, not a guess.

### Inventory

Parts get tied to work orders as they're used, so stock counts and billing stay in sync instead of drifting apart over a busy month.

### Scheduling and Staff

A visual calendar shows bay availability and technician assignments at a glance, which matters more than it seems once you're running more than one or two techs at once.

You can see the full breakdown on the [Garixo features page](https://www.garixo.com/features).

## What I'd Call the Real Pros

**It's built for one type of shop, not every type of shop.** Garixo doesn't try to be a platform for dealerships, fleet operations, and independent shops all at once. That focus is why the interface stays simple — every screen is designed around what a 1–10 tech shop actually does in a day.

**Flat pricing that doesn't punish growth.** Adding a technician or a second device doesn't quietly increase your bill. For a shop watching margins closely, that predictability is worth more than an extra feature you'll never open.

**Fast to set up, fast to learn.** There's no implementation project here. Most shops are running real jobs within a day, and a new hire can be shown the basics in an afternoon.

**Cloud-based means no server to maintain.** Data lives online with regular backups — accessible from any device, and not dependent on a single desktop computer that could fail.

## What I'd Call the Real Cons

I'd rather list these honestly than let you find them out after signing up.

**It's a newer, smaller platform than some established competitors.** Tools like Tekmetric or Mitchell 1 have been around longer, have larger user bases, and in some cases more third-party integrations. If a specific integration is a hard requirement for your shop, it's worth checking directly whether Garixo supports it yet.

**It's not built for multi-location or dealership-scale operations.** If you're running several locations or need heavy enterprise-style reporting across a large team, Garixo's simplicity — the same thing that makes it easy for a small shop — will feel limiting.

**It's web-based, not a dedicated native app.** Garixo works well in a mobile browser, but if your workflow depends specifically on a native mobile app experience, that's a different kind of tool than what Garixo offers today.

**Being cloud-based, it needs an internet connection.** For the vast majority of shops this is a non-issue, but a shop in an area with unreliable internet should factor that in.

None of these are secret — they're the direct tradeoff of building specifically for small independent shops instead of trying to serve everyone.
