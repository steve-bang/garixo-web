## Quick Answer: What Are Normal Auto Repair Shop Profit Margins?

For most small to mid-sized independent shops in the U.S.:

- **Gross profit margin:** 50%–65% overall
- **Labor gross margin:** 65%–75%
- **Parts gross margin:** 40%–55%
- **Net profit margin:** 10%–20% (after all expenses)

If you're below 10% net profit, your shop likely has pricing, labor efficiency, or overhead issues.

If you're consistently above 20%, you're running a very disciplined operation.

Now let’s go deeper so you can see where you stand — and how to improve.

## Understanding the Numbers That Actually Matter

When people talk about profit, they usually mix up three different things:

1. Gross profit
2. Net profit
3. Owner pay

If you don’t separate these clearly, your numbers won’t tell you the truth.

### Auto Shop Gross Margin

Your **auto shop gross margin** is revenue minus cost of goods sold (parts + direct labor).

Example:

- Monthly revenue: $120,000
- Parts cost: $35,000
- Technician wages (productive time only): $30,000

Gross profit = $55,000
Gross margin = 45.8%

That’s on the low side. Strong shops aim for **50–65% blended gross margin**.

The biggest lever here? Labor pricing and technician efficiency.

## What Is the Average Profit Margin Auto Repair Shop Owners See?

Across the U.S., the **average profit margin auto repair shop** owners report falls between 10% and 15% net profit.

That means:

If your shop does $1 million annually, a healthy net profit would be:

- $100,000–$150,000 after expenses
- Before taxes
- And ideally after paying yourself a fair market salary

This aligns with common **auto repair financial benchmarks US** operators use to measure performance.

Anything under 8% net profit is usually a warning sign.

## Breaking Down Shop Labor Margin Percentage

Labor is where your real profit lives.

Your **shop labor margin percentage** should typically land between 65% and 75%.

If it’s below 60%, one of these is happening:

- Your labor rate is too low
- Your techs aren’t flagging enough hours
- You’re discounting too often
- You’re eating time on diagnostics

Let’s say:

- You pay a tech $28/hour
- You charge $125/hour

On paper, that looks strong.

But if that tech is only flagging 30 hours in a 40-hour week, your effective margin drops fast.

Efficiency matters just as much as rate.

## Parts Markup: The Silent Profit Driver

Many shops lose thousands annually because they’re inconsistent on parts markup.

Healthy range:

- 40%–55% parts gross margin

Yet I regularly see shops operating at 30% or less without realizing it.

Common mistakes:

- Matching dealership pricing too closely
- Giving automatic discounts
- Forgetting small shop supply charges

## Repair Shop Overhead Costs: The Margin Killer

Your **repair shop overhead costs** should generally stay between 30%–40% of revenue.

This includes:

- Rent or mortgage
- Service advisors
- Utilities
- Insurance
- Software
- Office staff
- Marketing

If overhead creeps above 45%, your net profit disappears quickly.

That’s why operational visibility matters more than fancy marketing campaigns.
