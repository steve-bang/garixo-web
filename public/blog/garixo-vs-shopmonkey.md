**Quick answer:** Garixo and Shopmonkey both manage work orders, invoicing, and customer records for auto repair shops, but they're built around different priorities. Shopmonkey is built around a polished, customer-facing experience — digital vehicle inspections with photos, text updates, and a smooth approval flow. Garixo is built around simplicity and flat pricing for independent shops with 1–10 technicians who want the core workflow running fast, without a longer setup process. Neither is objectively better — it depends on what your shop actually needs day to day.

I'm Steve, Garixo's founder, so I'll say upfront where my bias sits. But this is meant to help you decide, not to talk you out of Shopmonkey if it's genuinely the better fit for your shop.

---

## Where the Two Platforms Actually Differ

### Customer Communication and Digital Inspections

This is Shopmonkey's clearest strength. It's built around a customer-facing experience — photo-based digital vehicle inspections, text updates as a job moves through the shop, and an approval flow that customers can act on from their phone. If getting customers to say yes to recommended work through a polished visual experience is a top priority, this is where Shopmonkey tends to shine.

Garixo covers the core communication a shop needs — status visibility, customer and vehicle history, invoicing — but it isn't built around a dedicated photo-based DVI feature the way Shopmonkey is. If DVI is a must-have for your shop specifically, it's worth testing that feature directly during a trial rather than assuming either platform covers it the way you picture it.

### Setup and Learning Curve

Garixo is built specifically for small independent shops, and that shows up most in setup time — most shops are running real jobs within a day, and a new service writer can learn the basics in an afternoon. Shopmonkey's broader feature set generally means a bit more configuration up front to get the workflow tuned the way you want it.

Neither is objectively "too complicated" — it's a question of whether you want more configuration in exchange for more built-in capability, or a faster path to a simple daily workflow. If you're not sure your shop needs a dedicated platform at all yet, [10 signs your auto repair shop is ready for management software](https://www.garixo.com/blog/signs-auto-repair-shop-needs-management-software) is worth a read first.

### Pricing Approach

Garixo publishes flat-rate pricing starting at **$79/month**, with no per-user fees — the bill doesn't change when you add a technician or a second device. Shopmonkey's pricing is generally quote-based and scales with shop size and feature tier, so the only accurate way to compare real cost is to request current pricing directly from Shopmonkey for your specific shop.

### Who Each One Is Built For

Garixo is built for independent shops with 1–10 technicians who want work orders, invoicing, inventory, and scheduling running well in one simple, flat-rate platform. See the full [Garixo features](https://www.garixo.com/features) breakdown.

Shopmonkey tends to suit shops — of various sizes — that put significant weight on the customer-facing experience and are comfortable with more setup to get a richer feature set tuned in.

## A Practical Way to Decide

Rather than picking from a comparison article, the more reliable test is:

1. **Name the one thing that would make you switch today.** If it's "customers can't see photos of the work we're recommending," that points toward Shopmonkey's strength. If it's "our invoicing takes too long and setup needs to be painless," that points toward what Garixo is built for.
2. **Trial both on real jobs**, not sample data — a demo environment won't show you the friction (or lack of it) that shows up on an actual busy day.
3. **Time your own onboarding.** If your service writer isn't comfortable within the first day or two, that's a real cost even though it's not on the invoice.
