**Quick answer:** A repair order (RO) is the document that authorizes and tracks a specific repair job from the moment a customer describes a problem through the labor and parts used to the final invoice. It's the single record that ties the whole job together — what the customer wanted, what was actually done, and what it cost. Every shop uses some version of a repair order, whether it's a paper ticket or a digital work order inside shop management software.

---

## What Is a Repair Order, Exactly?

A repair order is created the moment a vehicle comes in for service. It starts with the customer's complaint — "brakes are squeaking," "check engine light is on" — and grows as the job progresses: diagnosis notes, parts pulled, labor hours logged, and any additional work the technician finds and the customer approves along the way.

By the time the job is done, the repair order holds a complete record of the work: what was requested, what was found, what was done about it, and what it should cost.

---

## Repair Order vs. Estimate: What's the Difference?

An **estimate** is a proposed price given _before_ work is authorized — it's a prediction, not a record of what happened. Once the customer approves the estimate, it typically becomes (or gets attached to) the repair order, which then tracks the actual work as it's performed. If the job changes mid-repair — a tech finds something extra — the repair order is where that gets documented and re-approved, not the original estimate.

---

## Repair Order vs. Invoice: What's the Difference?

A **repair order** is created before and during the job to authorize and track the work as it happens. An **invoice** is generated after the work is complete — it's specifically the bill the customer pays.

In a lot of shops still running on paper, these end up as two separate documents, which is exactly where charges get lost: a repair order gets closed out, but something that happened during the job never makes it onto the invoice because it wasn't re-entered. In [digital work order systems](https://www.garixo.com/blog/digital-work-orders-auto-repair-shop), the invoice builds directly from the repair order, so nothing has to be retyped — and nothing quietly falls through the gap between the two documents.

## What Should Be on a Repair Order?

A complete repair order should include:

- **Customer information** — name, phone number, and preferred contact method
- **Vehicle details** — year, make, model, VIN or license plate, and current mileage
- **Customer's stated complaint** — in their own words, not just the technician's interpretation
- **Technician notes and diagnosis** — what was actually found once the vehicle was inspected
- **Parts used** — with cost and markup applied
- **Labor performed** — hours or flat-rate time, tied to a specific rate
- **Authorization** — a signature or digital approval before work proceeds, and again for any added work
- **Total cost** — which should match what eventually appears on the invoice

Missing any of these is usually where billing disputes and unbilled work start. If a repair order doesn't capture something a technician did, there's no clean path for it to end up on the invoice later.

## Why the Repair Order Matters More Than It Seems

It's easy to think of the repair order as paperwork — a formality between "customer describes problem" and "customer pays bill." In practice, it's the operational backbone of the shop: it's what a tech references mid-job, what a service writer uses to explain the bill, and what protects the shop if a customer questions a charge months later.

Shops that treat the repair order as a throwaway document tend to be the same shops wondering, at the end of the month, where the extra revenue went. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a repair order?

A repair order (RO) is the document that authorizes and tracks a specific repair job — from the customer's initial complaint through the labor and parts used to the final invoice. It's the record that ties every part of the job together.

### What is the difference between a repair order and an invoice?

A repair order is created before and during the job to authorize and track the work. An invoice is generated after the work is complete and is specifically the bill the customer pays. Many shops generate the invoice directly from the repair order once the job is closed.

### What is the difference between a repair order and an estimate?

An estimate is a proposed price given before work is authorized. Once the customer approves it, that estimate typically becomes — or gets attached to — the repair order, which then tracks the actual work as it happens.

### What should be included on a repair order?

At minimum, a repair order should include the customer's name and contact info, vehicle details (year, make, model, VIN or license plate, mileage), the customer's stated complaint, technician notes and diagnosis, parts and labor used, authorization signature or approval, and total cost.

### Do I need software to create repair orders, or can I use paper?

Paper repair orders work, but they make it easy to lose track of labor and parts that were performed but never billed. Digital repair orders reduce that risk by connecting labor and parts directly to the invoice as the job happens.
