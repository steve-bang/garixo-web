**Quick answer:** Tekmetric, Shopmonkey, and Garixo all manage work orders, invoicing, and customer records — but they're built for different priorities. Tekmetric leans toward reporting depth and repair-data integration. Shopmonkey leans toward customer communication and a polished digital inspection experience. Garixo leans toward simplicity and flat pricing for small independent shops running 1–10 technicians. The right one depends less on which is "best" and more on which matches how your shop actually operates.

I'm Steve, Garixo's founder, so take the Garixo section with the obvious grain of salt that implies — but I've tried to describe Tekmetric and Shopmonkey the way I'd want a competitor to describe Garixo: fairly, and based on what they're actually good at.

## Who Each Platform Is Really Built For

### Tekmetric

Tekmetric tends to appeal to shops that want strong reporting and deeper integration with repair and labor-time data. It's a solid fit if your shop leans on detailed analytics to manage technician performance and job profitability, and you don't mind a more involved setup to get there.

### Shopmonkey

Shopmonkey is generally known for a clean, modern interface and strong customer-facing communication — digital vehicle inspections with photos, text updates, and a smooth approval flow. It tends to suit shops that put a lot of weight on the customer experience side of the business.

### Garixo

Garixo is built specifically for independent shops with 1–10 technicians who want the core workflow — work orders, invoicing, customer history, inventory, scheduling — without a long setup process or a per-user pricing model that gets more expensive as the team grows. ## Where Tekmetric and Shopmonkey Have a Real Edge

To be fair about it: if your shop has grown past what a simple platform can handle — multiple locations, a large technician roster, or a need for very granular reporting — Tekmetric's analytics depth or Shopmonkey's customer communication tools may genuinely serve you better than a simpler platform. Neither is a bad choice. They're built for a wider range of shop sizes, which is exactly why they carry more setup and more surface area to learn.

If that's not your shop — if you're a 1–10 tech independent operation that wants the core workflow running well without a learning curve — that's the specific gap Garixo is built to fill. ## How to Actually Decide

Don't decide from a comparison article — including this one. The fastest real test:

1. **List your must-haves.** Not nice-to-haves — the two or three things that would make you switch today (unbilled labor, no-shows, slow invoicing, whatever it actually is).
2. **Run a free trial on real jobs**, not sample data. A demo environment hides the friction that only shows up on a busy Tuesday.
3. **Time your own setup.** If it takes your service writer more than a day to feel comfortable, factor that cost in — it's real, even if it's not on the invoice.

If unbilled labor or missed charges are part of what's pushing you to look at new software in the first place, [why your shop is losing $40,000 a year in unbilled labor](https://www.garixo.com/blog/unbilled-labor-auto-repair-shop) is worth reading alongside whichever platform you're evaluating.
